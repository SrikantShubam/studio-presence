# Content system — Instagram · YouTube · Journal

One escalating funnel, not three channels.

---

## The pillar

**UI/UX and website commentary.** Interior design studios are the primary subject matter.

This is the decision that makes everything else work. A teardown of an interior studio's website
*is* design commentary — so one content stream serves both audiences without splitting anything:

- **Studio owners** see: *he understands what my website is supposed to do*
- **Product/tech buyers** see: real design judgement **demonstrated**, not claimed

One feed. One journal. No category war, no diluted algorithm. And a far less crowded lane than AI
content, which everyone is already making.

---

## The unit: one story, four depths

Every piece of content is one **story**, expressed at four depths. One story per week produces four
assets from a single piece of thinking.

| Depth | Format | Length | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Instagram carousel | 4–6 slides | The hook and the shape of the argument. Ends: "full breakdown, link in bio" |
| 2 | Reel / YouTube Short | 30–60s | Same story in motion. Screen-recorded scroll with text callouts |
| 3 | YouTube video | 6–10 min | The demonstration — actually doing the teardown or rebuild |
| 4 | Journal post | 1,200–2,000 words | The reference version. What gets sent in DMs and what eventually ranks |

⚠️ **No on-camera face or voice** (per `05-outreach-sales-playbook.md`). Every video is a screen
recording with on-screen captions or TTS narration. This suits teardowns perfectly — the screen *is*
the subject, and a face would only get in the way.

---

## The escalation mechanic

Not one post per story. **A three-part series across the week, then the payoff.**

| Day | Post | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Part 1 | "This studio's website has three problems. First: the hero." |
| Wed | Part 2 | "Problem two: you can't find the phone number." |
| Fri | Part 3 | "Problem three: the portfolio is doing nothing." |
| Sun | Payoff | "We rebuilt it. Full teardown and before/after on the journal →" |

Why this beats a single post:
- Four posts from one piece of work
- Builds anticipation instead of asking for a click cold
- **Trains the audience to expect the link** — by week three, people wait for Sunday
- Each part is independently saveable and shareable

---

## The funnel

```
Instagram carousel  →  Journal post  →  /studios  →  WhatsApp
   30 seconds           5 minutes       the offer    conversion
    no ask             proves depth      one CTA     one action
```

**One next action per surface.** Never two competing CTAs on the same page or post. The carousel's
only job is the link. The journal post's only job is `/studios`. The `/studios` page's only job is
WhatsApp.

---

## Formats, ranked by leverage

| Format | Example | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Teardown** ⭐ | "This studio's site: three things costing them clients" | Highest leverage in the system. Works at all four depths, doubles as a warm outreach opener, and generates the next post |
| **Rebuild / before-after** ⭐ | "We rebuilt it — here's the difference" | The money format. Inherently visual, instantly legible with zero explanation |
| **Comparison** | "₹15,000 site vs ₹50,000 site: what actually differs" · "Wix vs custom for a studio" | Highest-performing format on Instagram for this vertical. Needs no context to understand |
| **Pattern** | "Why every good studio site puts the phone number in the same place" | Demonstrates judgement. This is the format that pulls the product/design audience in |
| **Mistake** | "The one thing every interior studio website gets wrong" | Strong hook, easy to serialise across three parts |
| **Self-audit** | "Five things to check on your own site right now" | Saveable and shareable. Reliably generates inbound DMs |

### The teardown is the atomic unit

Twenty minutes of work produces:
- 4 Instagram posts
- 1 Reel
- 1 YouTube video
- 1 journal post
- **A warm outreach opener** — *"We do free teardowns of studio websites. Want yours?"*
- The subject of the next teardown, via whoever replies

Nothing else in the system compounds like this.

⚠️ **Never name and shame.** Tear down a site, don't humiliate a business. Blur the studio name in
public content unless they've agreed — or better, tear down *your own* earlier work, or a
composite. The moment a studio owner sees a peer publicly mocked, every prospect in that city stops
trusting you. Send the named version privately to the studio itself; publish the anonymised one.

## Cadence

**One story per week** = 4 IG posts + 1 Reel + 1 YouTube + 1 journal post.

If that isn't sustainable solo, drop to fortnightly and say so on the page. What matters more than
volume:

- **Never let the newest journal post go older than six weeks** during active outreach. A journal
  that stopped four months ago signals abandonment — precisely the fear the whole business is
  selling against.
- **Don't date every post the same week.** A batch of posts all published on one day reads as a
  campaign dump. Space them.

## How content feeds outreach

The journal is not primarily an SEO asset in the first six months. Its job is to **survive the
credibility check**: the intern DMs a studio, the studio Googles Vector Veda, and the journal either
says *these people know this business* or it doesn't.

That means posts are outreach collateral. Send, don't pitch:

> Hi [Name], I'm Srikant from Vector Veda. I wrote this on what studio websites actually cost in
> India — most agencies won't publish numbers, so I did. Thought it might be useful. [link]

No ask. They read it, they see you're real, and frequently **they** raise price first. A useful link
is also far less likely to be reported as spam than a sales message, which protects the account
limits that outreach depends on.

Full post→moment mapping in `journal-content-plan.md`.

## Instagram specifics

- **Feed stays single-vertical.** Interiors only. Two audiences on one feed confuses both the
  algorithm and the follower base. The halo effect happens on the *website*, where a visitor
  browsing the journal encounters the full range of work on their own.
- **Carousels over single images.** More dwell time, more saves, and they suit a serialised argument.
- **First slide is the whole battle.** It must work with no context and no sound.
- **Link in bio → the journal post**, not the homepage. Let the post carry the quiet link to `/studios`.
- **Engage before you DM.** Comment on a studio's post before messaging them — it looks human
  because it is, and it materially reduces the restriction risk covered in `intern-kit.md` §4.

---

## YouTube specifics

- Screen recordings only. No face, no voice — captions or TTS.
- **Title for the search, not the joke.** "Interior designer website review" beats "This site is a
  disaster."
- 6–10 minutes. Long enough to demonstrate real work, short enough to finish.
- Every video description links the journal post; every journal post embeds the video.
- YouTube is the **slowest** channel to pay off and the most credible when it does. Treat it as a
  deposit, not a campaign.

---

## What not to do

- **Don't publish AI/product content during the interiors push.** A prospect mid-credibility-check
  landing on fintech content dilutes exactly what `/studios` exists to fix. Queue those; publish
  after the vertical is established.
- **Don't gate anything behind an email form.** You're proving you're real, not running a funnel. A
  gate at this stage costs more trust than it captures addresses.
- **Don't optimise for SEO first.** The keywords that matter are low-volume and six-plus months out.
  Outreach doesn't wait for them.
- **Don't link DMs to the homepage.** Always to the specific post.
- **Don't post three formats of the same story on the same day.** The escalation is the point.
