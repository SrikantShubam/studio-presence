# Sales intern kit

Everything the intern needs to do the job. Read this fully before the first outreach.

This document is also the **documented learning component** of the internship — the training,
product education, and structured feedback described here are what make this a genuine internship
rather than disguised employment.

---

## 1. What Vector Veda actually sells

We build websites for interior design studios in Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities. ₹15,000 for the standard
package, ₹25,000 for the larger one, ₹45,000+ for custom. **Those are the only three options — there
is no cheap version.** If someone can't do ₹12,000, they're not our customer, and that's fine.

**What makes us different — memorise this, it's the whole pitch:**

Anyone can build a website. A college student, a Fiverr freelancer, someone's nephew. The problem
interior designers have already experienced is that those people **disappear**. They take the money,
build something, and stop replying. There's no invoice, no contract, no one to call when something
breaks.

We are a registered firm. We give a written contract, a proper GST invoice, and a real business to
come back to. That is what the client is actually buying — not pixels, reliability.

**Second thing: they don't need a website, they need enquiries.** Never sell "a website." Sell what
it does — someone searches for an interior designer in their area, finds them instead of a
competitor, and messages them on WhatsApp.

## 2. Your job

**You do not close deals. You find and qualify people who want to talk, and hand them over.**

That's the entire job, and it's harder than it sounds. Specifically:

1. Work your assigned segment of the lead list
2. Reach out across WhatsApp, Instagram, and calls
3. Have real conversations — find out what their situation actually is
4. Record and document every conversation
5. Escalate genuinely interested leads to Srikant
6. Keep the tracker accurate

**You never:** quote a price below ₹15,000, promise a feature, commit to a timeline, or negotiate.
If any of those come up, the answer is: *"Let me get Srikant on this, he handles the specifics."*
That's not a weakness in the pitch — handing off to the founder is a signal that they're being taken
seriously.

## 4. How to reach out — and the rules that keep us out of trouble

We are not a spam operation. Getting our numbers blocked or our Instagram restricted destroys the
lead list permanently, and the list is the business's most valuable asset. **Volume is not the
metric. Qualified conversations are.**

### WhatsApp — primary channel
- Manual, one-to-one messages only. Never bulk tools, never copy-paste blasts.
- **Personalise every message.** Mention their actual studio name and something real about them.
- If someone says stop, or doesn't want to be contacted — stop immediately, mark dead, move on.
- Roughly 20–30 genuine personalised messages a day is sustainable. Bursts get flagged.

### Instagram DM — secondary
- **Hard limit: 15–20 DMs per day, spread across hours.** Never in bursts.
- New accounts get restricted after as few as 5–10 cold DMs. First restriction is 7 days, second is
  30 days. That's your income gone for a month — take the limit seriously.
- Engage with a post before DMing. It looks human because it is.

### Calls — only for warm leads
- Call people who have already replied. Cold-dialling scraped numbers is a legal grey area in India
  under TRAI rules and gets numbers flagged.
- Best window: 11am–1pm, 4pm–7pm. Avoid Monday morning and Saturday evening.

### Email — lowest priority
Legal but low response. Use only when no other channel exists.

## 5. Scripts

Scripts are starting points, not recitations. If you sound like you're reading, you've lost.
Full versions with objection handling: `outreach-scripts.md`.

### WhatsApp first message
> Hi [Name], I'm [Your name] from Vector Veda — we build websites for interior designers.
>
> I came across [Studio Name] and had a look at your Instagram — the [specific real detail] work
> looked great. But I couldn't find a website for you when I searched.
>
> I put together a quick preview of what one could look like for you. Want me to send it?

Then, once they say yes, send the Loom + watermarked link.

### The opening question that does the most work
> "When someone in [their city] wants an interior designer and searches on Google — what comes up
> for you right now?"

Let them answer. Most will realise mid-sentence that the answer is "nothing" or "my competitor."
You don't have to make the argument; the question makes it.

### The ROI line
> "Your average project is what, ₹2 lakh? ₹3? So if this brings you even two clients in a year,
> it's paid for itself several times over. That's the whole maths."

Let them do the arithmetic out loud. People believe their own numbers, not yours.

## 6. What counts as a qualified escalation

Not every reply is a lead. Escalate only when **all four** are true:

1. They're the **decision-maker** (owner or partner, not an employee)
2. They've shown **specific interest** — asked about price, timeline, or what's included
3. There is **no hard blocker** — not "we already have a website we're happy with"
4. You've captured the **four minimum fields**: business name, locality, what they do, contact number

Escalations that don't meet all four waste the founder's time and dilute the pipeline. Quality of
escalation is what the base pay is measuring.

**How to escalate:** update the tracker, then message Srikant with — studio name, city, contact,
what they said in their own words, what they seem to want, which tier fits, and any concern raised.

Every lead, every time. This is not admin overhead — it's what stops us contacting the same studio
twice, which makes us look exactly like the spam operation we're claiming not to be.

| Their words | **Quote them, don't paraphrase.** This is the most valuable column in the sheet |

### The dead-lead rule
No response after **2 follow-ups over 2 weeks** → mark dead, move on. Do not keep poking. Fresh
qualified leads beat re-warming cold ones, every time.

## 8. Recording calls

Record every call. Two reasons: it's how you improve, and it's how objections get turned into
better scripts for everyone.

- **Tell them you're recording.** "Just so you know, I record calls for training — is that alright?"
  Nobody has ever said no, and asking is both legally cleaner and a trust signal.
- Save with a consistent name: `YYYY-MM-DD_StudioName.mp3`
- Link it in the tracker
- Flag any call where you got stuck — those are the useful ones to review together

## 10. What good looks like after one month

- 300–500 genuine personalised outreaches
- 30–60 replies
- 10–20 real conversations
- 3–8 qualified escalations
- A tracker that needs no correction
- Zero blocked numbers, zero Instagram restrictions

If replies are healthy but escalations are low, the problem is in the conversation — we'll work on
the script. If replies are low, the problem is the message or the targeting. **We diagnose these
separately, and neither one is treated as your fault.** They're information about the process.

---

## 11. Ask, don't guess

If you're unsure about anything — price, feature, timeline, whether something's included — ask.
A wrong answer given confidently to a prospect costs far more than a five-minute delay. Nobody is
annoyed by questions. Everybody is annoyed by a promise we can't keep.
