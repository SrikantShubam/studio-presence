# Outreach scripts and objection handling

Starting points, not recitations. If it sounds read, it's lost. Adapt the wording, keep the shape.

Compliance rules and daily limits: `intern-kit.md` §4. They are not optional.

---

## Segment first

Same product, different opening. Sending the wrong one wastes the lead.

| Segment | Signal | Opening angle | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Established** (5–15 yrs) | Strong work, weak/no site, doesn't feel lead pain | Prestige — "your work deserves better presentation" | T1 / T2 |
| **Growing** (2–6 yrs) | Active Instagram, relies on DMs and referrals, feels the pain | Lost leads — "how many enquiries did you lose track of?" | T2 / T3 |
| **New solo** (0–2 yrs) | Little presence, very price-sensitive | Credibility — "look established without the agency price" | T1 |

Prioritise **Growing**. They have the pain, the money, and the shortest cycle.

## WhatsApp — first message

### Growing studio
> Hi [Name], I'm [Your name] from Vector Veda — we build websites for interior designers.
>
> Came across [Studio Name] — the [specific real project/style you actually saw] work looked great.
> But when I searched for interior designers in [locality], I couldn't find a site for you.
>
> I made a quick preview of what one could look like for you. Want me to send it?

### Established studio
> Hi [Name], I'm [Your name] from Vector Veda.
>
> I've been looking at interior designers in [city] and [Studio Name]'s work stood out — [specific
> detail]. Nine years of projects like that and there's no website showing them properly.
>
> I put together a preview. Can I send it across?

### New solo designer
> Hi [Name], [Your name] here from Vector Veda — we build websites for interior designers.
>
> Saw your work on Instagram. When you're starting out the hardest part is looking established
> enough that people trust you with a ₹3 lakh project.
>
> Made you a quick preview — want to see it?

**Rules:** one specific real detail, always. No "I loved your work" — that reads as a template
because it is one. Never send the link unrequested; ask first, so the reply is a small yes.

## Instagram DM

Same content, shorter. Engage with a post first.

> Hi [Name] — [Your name] from Vector Veda. We build websites for interior designers.
>
> Your [specific post/project] caught my eye. Noticed there's no site linked in your bio — made you
> a quick preview of what one could look like. Want me to send it?

Max 15–20/day, spread across hours.

## After they say yes

> Here you go — [watermarked link]
>
> Quick 60-second walkthrough: [Loom link]
>
> This is a preview on our domain, so it's watermarked. If you like it, it goes on your own domain
> with your own email. From ₹15,000, live in two days.
>
> Anything you'd change?

Ending on a question, not a price, keeps the conversation open.

## The call

### Opening
> Hi [Name], [Your name] from Vector Veda — is this a good time for two minutes?

If no: *"No problem, when suits you?"* Then actually call then.

### The question that does the work
> "When someone in [city] wants an interior designer and searches Google — what comes up for you
> right now?"

Then **stop talking.** Most owners answer their own objection mid-sentence. You don't need to make
the argument; the question makes it.

### Follow-ups
- "Where do most of your enquiries come from at the moment?"
- "How many projects a year would you like to be doing that you're not?"
- "Have you tried JustDial or anything like that? How did that go?"

That last one is usually a floodgate. Let them vent — you learn everything and they feel heard.

### The ROI close
> "Your average project is around ₹2–3 lakh, right? So if this gets you two clients in a year, it's
> paid for itself several times. That's the whole maths — it doesn't need to be more complicated."

Let them do the arithmetic aloud. People believe their own numbers.

### Handing off
> "The specifics on pricing and what's included — let me get Srikant on this, he handles that side.
> Can I set up a quick call this week?"

## Objections

**"How much?"** *(the most common, and it comes early)*
> ₹15,000 for the standard package, ₹25,000 for the bigger one, and custom above that. Everything's
> on our site — we don't do the "contact for pricing" thing.

Never discount here. You aren't authorised to and it destroys the anchor.

**"Too expensive."**
> Fair. What were you expecting? — [listen] — For context, JustDial runs ₹6,000 to ₹50,000 a year,
> every year. This is once, and it's yours. And if it lands two projects it's paid back many times.

If they genuinely can't do ₹12k, say so plainly and leave the door open:
*"Then we're probably not the right fit right now — and I'd rather say that than build you something
half-done. If the budget changes, I'm here."*

**There is no cheap version to fall back on.** That's deliberate. Walking away protects the price for
every other studio in the city, and they talk to each other.

**"I already have a website."**
> Makes sense. Is it bringing you enquiries? — [listen] — If it's working, honestly you don't need
> us. If it's sitting there not doing much, that's worth a look.

Walking away from a bad fit is a strong move and it lands as honest, because it is.

**"I'll think about it."**
> Of course. What's the part you want to think about — the price, or whether it'll actually bring
> work in?

Names the real objection instead of leaving it buried.

**"I don't have time for this."**
> That's kind of the point — we need four things from you and photos you already have on your phone.
> Two days, and you're not involved in the middle.

**"My nephew/friend can build one."**
> He probably can. The thing we hear a lot is that those sites get built and then nobody's around
> when something needs changing. We're a registered firm — contract, GST invoice, someone to call
> next year. That's really what you're paying for.

**"How do I know you're legitimate?"**
> Completely fair — you shouldn't take my word for it. We're a registered firm, you get a written
> contract and a proper invoice, and you see the site working before you pay anything. If it's not
> right, you owe nothing.

**"Send me details on email."**
Often a soft brush-off. Test it:
> Sure. Also — want me to just send the preview on WhatsApp? Takes 10 seconds to look at.

**"I get all my work through referrals."**
> That's the best kind. Most people who get referred still search the name before calling — that's
> usually the moment a site matters. It backs up the referral.

## Follow-up sequence

| When | Message |
|---|---|
| Day 3 | > Hi [Name], did you get a chance to look at the preview? Happy to change anything on it. |
| Day 10 | > Hi [Name] — last one from me. Preview's still up: [link]. If it's not the right time, no problem at all — just say and I'll leave you be. |
| Day 14 | Mark dead. Move on. |

Two follow-ups over two weeks. Then stop. The permission to say no in the second message
consistently gets more replies than pressure does.

---

## Things that lose deals
- Sending the link before they've agreed to see it
- Generic praise with no specific detail
- Quoting below ₹15,000
- Promising a feature or timeline you can't confirm
- Arguing with an objection instead of asking about it
- Chasing past two follow-ups
- Talking through the silence after a good question
