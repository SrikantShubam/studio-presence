# Partnership deed — clause checklist

> ⚠️ Draft with a CA or lawyer. This is the list of what must be in it and why — not the deed
> itself. Several of these clauses forfeit real money if they're missing or vague.

**Firm:** Vector Veda
**Partners:** Srikant (70%) and father (30%)
**Structure decision:** partnership firm, **not** LLP — see §2.

---

## 1. Must be stated explicitly

| # | Clause | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Firm name, place of business, commencement date | Basic constitution |
| 2 | **Profit-sharing ratio: 70% / 30%** | Must be explicit. Silence defaults to equal shares, which is not what's intended |
| 3 | Loss-sharing ratio | State it separately even if identical — don't leave it inferred |
| 4 | Capital contribution by each partner | Establishes the base for interest on capital |
| 5 | **Interest on partners' capital — up to 12% p.a.** | ⚠️ **The rate and the computation method must both be stated.** A deed that is silent, or vague about how the capital base is computed, gets the deduction disallowed entirely under §40(b) |
| 6 | **Partner remuneration clause** | ⚠️ Include it even though the current plan is to draw **nil** remuneration (see §3). A deed silent on remuneration cannot pay it later without being redrafted. State the §40(b) statutory limits as the ceiling and allow the partners to fix a lower amount, including zero, by mutual agreement |
| 7 | Who is a **working partner** | Remuneration is only deductible when paid to a working partner. Name them |
| 8 | Banking, signing authority, drawings | Operational clarity |
| 9 | Duration — "at will" or fixed term | "At will" is normal for a two-person firm |
| 10 | Admission, retirement, death of a partner | Without this, a partnership legally dissolves on a partner exiting. Given the age gap between the partners here, this clause is not academic |
| 11 | Dispute resolution | Arbitration clause, seat named |
| 12 | Accounting year and book-keeping responsibility | Even under presumptive taxation, name who is responsible |

## 2. Why a partnership firm and not an LLP

**LLPs are not eligible for Section 44AD or 44ADA presumptive taxation.**

That single fact decides the structure at this revenue level. Presumptive taxation is worth roughly
₹2 lakh a year at ₹20L revenue against the alternative — an LLP would trade that away for limited
liability that a two-person website business doesn't yet need.

Revisit LLP or Pvt Ltd when any of these become true:
- Turnover approaches the ₹3 crore presumptive ceiling
- Contracts start carrying meaningful data, IP, or indemnity risk
- Outside investment or a third partner enters
- Roughly ₹50L–1cr turnover

Until then: unlimited personal liability is the accepted trade, and it's mitigated by §12 of the
client agreement capping liability at fees paid.

## 3. Remuneration: keep the clause, draw nil

The plan is to take **no partner remuneration and no interest on capital**, and to take everything as
profit share instead.

**Why:** under 44AD the firm is taxed on 6% deemed profit, and a partner's share of firm profit is
exempt in the partner's hands under §10(2A). Paying remuneration instead would convert exempt income
into taxable salary — and with ₹6L of rental income already using ₹4.2L of the ₹12L §87A rebate
headroom, that salary would push past the rebate ceiling and create personal tax where there is
currently none.

**But the clause still goes in the deed**, set to nil by mutual agreement, so the option exists
without a redraft if circumstances change.

**Consequence of drawing nil:** §194T (10% TDS on partner remuneration above ₹20,000/year) doesn't
apply. One fewer compliance obligation.

⚠️ Ask the CA to confirm the §10(2A) treatment specifically where the firm is assessed under 44AD
and actual profit substantially exceeds the 6% deemed figure. The mainstream position is that the
full share stays exempt, but this assumption carries the entire structure — get it confirmed once,
in writing.

## 4. Registration and related

| Item | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Partnership deed on stamp paper | To do | Under ₹5,000 to draft |
| Firm PAN | To do | Required before GST and bank account |
| Firm registration with Registrar of Firms | Optional | Not mandatory, but an unregistered firm cannot sue to enforce contracts — worth doing given the business runs on client contracts |
| Current account in the firm's name | To do | Needed for Razorpay/Cashfree |
| **GST registration** | In process | ₹20L services threshold |
| **TAN** | To do | ₹77, 3–15 days. **Needs firm PAN first.** Required before the first intern commission payment |
| **Udyam / MSME registration** | Done | Unlocks MSMED 45-day payment protection with 3× RBI-rate compound interest, enforceable via the Samadhaan portal. Also speeds Google Business Profile suspension appeals. ⚠️ **Verify it's against the firm's PAN** — the protection attaches to the registered entity, so a Udyam under a proprietorship or personal PAN won't cover the firm's client invoices |
| Professional tax | Check | Karnataka ₹2,496/yr; verify the Bihar slab on the state portal |
| Trademark on "Vector Veda" | Later | Udyam gives a 50% fee subsidy |

## 5. Order of operations

1. Partnership deed drafted and executed
2. Firm PAN
3. Current account
4. GST registration
5. TAN
6. Udyam
7. Razorpay/Cashfree onboarding
8. Only then: first client invoice

Steps 1–3 gate everything else. Nothing about outreach or product build depends on this sequence —
build in parallel, but do not invoice before step 8.
