01

THE
BRIEF

A family of four had just taken possession of a 1,320 sq ft flat off Boring Road. Two working parents, two school-going children, one grandmother visiting for months at a time — and a builder shell with a dark corridor and a kitchen closed off behind a wall.

The brief was practical rather than aspirational. They wanted storage that could absorb a decade of accumulation, a kitchen where two people could cook together during festivals, and a home that would still look right when the furniture inevitably got moved around. The budget was fixed at ten lakh, and they were honest that it could not stretch.

We spent the first two sittings measuring, photographing and listening — no drawings, no mood boards. Most of what shaped this project came out of that conversation: the grandmother needed a room she could shut, the children shared study hours, and the family ate at the table every night.

02

OUR
APPROACH

Rather than spread a thin budget across every surface, we put the money into three decisions and kept everything else deliberately plain — white walls, a single laminate family, no false ceilings except where wiring demanded them.

A

THE KITCHEN WALL CAME DOWN

A half-height counter replaced it. The dark corridor became the brightest part of the flat, and two people can now work either side of the hob without colliding.

B

STORAGE IN THE DEAD SPACE

Every corridor wall, bed base and window seat carries concealed storage — 214 cubic feet of it, built in commercial ply so it survives Patna's humidity.

C

ONE MATERIAL, REPEATED

A single warm oak laminate runs through joinery in all five rooms. It cost less by volume, it ages evenly, and a damaged panel can be matched years from now.

03

THE
OUTCOME

Handover was on day 45, three days later than the dated schedule — a delay caused by a marble slab that arrived cracked and had to be re-cut. The final bill came in at ₹9.4 lakh, inside the range they had set, with material invoices handed over in a folder.

A year on, the joinery has held through one full monsoon and the family has moved the sofa twice without the room falling apart — which was, in the end, the actual brief. They have since sent us two neighbours in the same building.
