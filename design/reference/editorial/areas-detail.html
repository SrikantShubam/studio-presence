Our studio is on the second floor above the crossing, so most of this work is within walking distance — which changes how we run a site here.

Boring Road is two kinds of housing sitting side by side. The lanes off the main road — Boring Canal Road, the stretch towards Rajapur Pul — are full of 1990s and 2000s builder flats: solid 1,200 to 1,500 sq ft three-bedroom units with high ceilings, generous balconies, and kitchens walled off behind a door because that is how they were drawn thirty years ago. Almost every job we take in those buildings starts with the same two questions: can that kitchen wall come down, and can the balcony be brought inside.

The newer towers along the main road are a different brief entirely — 900 to 1,100 sq ft, lower ceilings, better plumbing, and a society office that will only allow drilling between ten and five on weekdays. We plan those sites around the lift schedule and cut everything in our workshop, because you cannot run a cutting machine in that lobby.

What people here ask for is fairly consistent: storage that hides a decade of accumulation, a study corner for children in coaching, and a drawing room presentable enough for the guests who arrive without calling. Budgets usually land between ₹8 and ₹14 lakh for a full flat.
